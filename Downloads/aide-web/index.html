        Built on Australian evidence.

        The numbers behind the late-afternoon window, the rules AIDE is designed around, and exactly what AIDE does and doesn't do.

        The problem in numbers

        22.5%

        of falls in 25 Australian aged care homes happened between 2pm and 6pm, more than in any other four hours of the day.

        Wabe et al., Int J Qual Health Care 2022. 27,878 falls. [1]

        ~$2,500

        average cost of a fall injury in Australian aged care. Fall injuries are about 20% of annual spending per resident.

        Okafor et al., Injury Prevention 2025. [2]

        33→23%

        of residents living with dementia given an antipsychotic, 2018 to 2022. Progress, but still double the rate for residents without dementia.

        Raban et al., JAMDA 2025. [3]

        215/44

        mandatory average care minutes per resident per day, including 44 registered nurse minutes.

        Department of Health, Disability and Ageing. [4]

        Built around the Aged Care Act 2024

        Since 1 November 2025, restrictive practices must be a last resort, with alternatives tried and documented first.

        • Alternatives tried and documented firstEach routine started is a dated record of non-drug support.
        • Evidence for behaviour support plansWhat was tried, when, and what helped, ready for plan review.
        • Strengthened Quality Standards, Standard 5Supports the clinical care standard's expectations for behaviour support.
        • Less double documentationRecords flow to the care team without re-typing. [[TODO: confirm integrations]]

        What AIDE is, and isn't.

        AIDE is behaviour support and documentation software. It does not diagnose, assess, predict or triage any condition. It does not recommend or change medication, and it does not replace clinical judgement. Clinical decisions stay with your care team.
